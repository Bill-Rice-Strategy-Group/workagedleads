The five fields that decide whether you can measure anything

If your CRM captures only these five on every lead, you can answer the question that matters: which batch paid for itself? (1) Batch ID: source, vertical, and purchase date in one tag. (2) First-attempt timestamp. (3) Attempt count. (4) Final disposition. (5) Closed revenue. Divide revenue by what the batch cost and you have ROI per batch. Without the batch ID, every lead blurs together, and you can't tell which purchase to repeat.

Disposition codes that tell you something useful

Test every code with one question: does it trigger a different next action? If two codes lead to the same follow-up, merge them. A workable starter set of about eight: Appointment Set, Callback Requested, Not Interested, Not Qualified, Wrong Number, Disconnected, Opt-Out/DNC, and Unreached – Cadence Complete. Each one tells you whether to keep working the lead, recycle it, or suppress it permanently. 'Other' and 'Follow up' tell you nothing, so retire them.

Why 'no answer' is not a disposition

No answer is the result of one attempt, not an outcome for the lead. Log it as an attempt (it bumps your attempt count) and leave the disposition open. Then set a rule for when the lead earns a real disposition. As a starting point, try six attempts spread across at least three different time windows (morning, midday, evening) over two to three weeks. When that's done, mark the lead 'Unreached – Cadence Complete' and give it a recycle date 60 to 90 days out. Otherwise 'no answer' becomes a graveyard of leads nobody decided anything about.

Industry Insight

Consent rules keep moving, so your suppression process has to be steady

In January 2025, the Eleventh Circuit vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule, which would have restricted how leads could be shared across sellers. It's a good example of how quickly the rules around purchased leads can shift. The baseline obligations haven't gone anywhere: Do-Not-Call scrubbing, honoring opt-outs, and compliance with state-level calling laws. The practical takeaway as you scale is to make scrubbing and opt-out suppression a fixed step in your workflow, and to confirm current requirements with your own counsel.
