Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

Cap your dials and spend them on people who already moved

This week, set a hard ceiling of 25 to 30 dials a day and give them only to leads who have done something: replied to an email, opened it more than once, or clicked a link. Put everyone else on email only. A short, focused call block on Tuesday or Wednesday morning will do more than a six-hour slog into voicemail. Skip Thanksgiving Day itself entirely.

Test the days either side of the holiday

The Wednesday before and the Friday after tend to be good days to reach people: they're home or between plans, they have their phone in hand, and they aren't in back-to-back meetings. Treat that as a hypothesis, not a guarantee, and track your own answer rate for those days against a normal week. A low-pressure opener works well: "Hi [Name], hope the holiday's treating you well. I'll keep this to 30 seconds. You'd looked into [product] a while back and I wanted to see if it's still on your radar." If they're busy, offer a specific callback time in the first week of December.

Set up December before you take time off

Block 90 minutes before you log off and sort your sheet into three tabs: replied, opened but silent, and no engagement. Schedule a 3-email sequence to the second and third tabs, spaced 4 to 5 days apart, then put your first-week-of-December call blocks on the calendar with the names of everyone who has replied. Add an out-of-office that says when you'll be back and when you return calls, so nobody replying over the holiday is left wondering.

Industry Insight

Holiday travel and the 8am-to-9pm rule

Under the FCC's TCPA rules, telemarketing calls are only permitted between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time. Holiday travel makes this easy to trip over, because a lead's area code often no longer tells you where they are. The practical principle is to schedule against the time zone of the number's home area, stay well inside the window, and treat any lead who says they're travelling as a reason to move your call, not push through it.
