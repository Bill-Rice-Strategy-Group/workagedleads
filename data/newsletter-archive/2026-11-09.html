Weekly strategies for sales professionals — by Bill Rice

Ask most buyers how they judge a lead source and they'll quote a price per lead. It's the easiest number to see, and it tells you the least about whether the leads made you money.

The number that decides it is your cost per acquisition: what it cost you, all-in, to close one sale. This issue is about measuring it honestly, including the hours you never put a price on. You'll also get a way to compare aged and real-time leads on equal terms, and the one monthly report that shows whether your lead spend is working.

There's a bigger reason to care. A pipeline only produces sales while there are leads in front of you, and the most common way it fails is running dry. A steady, affordable restock you can sustain month after month usually beats big, sporadic buys. Knowing your CPA is how you find out what you can sustain.

Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

Put your unbilled hours in the CPA

Use this formula: (lead spend + data cleaning and scrubbing + dialer/CRM/texting costs + your labor hours × what an hour of your time is worth) ÷ sales closed. Say you spend 20 hours working a batch and your time is worth $50 an hour. That's $1,000 of cost that never shows up on an invoice, and most buyers leave it out. Track hours with a simple log for one batch and you'll see which sources are cheap on paper and expensive in practice.

Compare aged and real-time on break-even, not price

Lead price can't settle this, so use one comparison. Calculate the all-in cost per lead for each source, labor included. Aged wins on CPA whenever its close rate is above the real-time close rate multiplied by (aged all-in cost ÷ real-time all-in cost). Because aged leads cost a fraction of what fresh ones do, the close rate you need to break even is usually far below what you'd guess. Run both sources over the same 90-day window with the same cadence and the same time accounting, or the comparison tells you nothing.

The one monthly report: a batch cohort sheet

Build one row per lead batch with these columns: leads received, leads that got the full cadence, contacts made, appointments set, sales closed, all-in spend, CPA, and days to first sale. A healthy report has three traits. Nearly every lead gets the full cadence, because untouched leads are the most expensive kind. CPA holds steady or falls from batch to batch. Older batches keep producing sales in months two and three. Set your own baseline from your first three months rather than chasing someone else's benchmark.

Industry Insight

Compliance is a recurring cost line, so count it in your CPA

Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule safe harbor, a seller has to scrub against a version of the Do-Not-Call registry obtained no more than 31 days earlier, and scrubbing is only one of the safe harbor's conditions. That makes scrubbing a monthly operating cost for anyone dialing purchased data, not a one-time setup fee. Include it in your all-in CPA, and budget for it before you size your next restock.
