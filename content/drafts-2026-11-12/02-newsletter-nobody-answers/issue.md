**Subject:** 70 dials, zero conversations

**Preview text:** Why aged leads stopped answering, and how to pick who to call.

## Intro

A reader wrote to us last month with a problem a lot of you will recognize. He's a final expense agent, 69, and he used to reach 5 people for every 10 calls. Now he sometimes makes 70 or 80 calls in a day and talks to no one. He wants to work two or three days a week. He's working five.

Calling at better times won't fix it. Most people now ignore numbers they don't know. And a number that makes a lot of unanswered calls gets labeled "Spam Likely" by the phone companies, so even fewer people pick up. Dialing harder makes the label worse.

The fix is to change the order. Email goes first and finds out who's still interested. You call those people, from a number they're starting to recognize. This issue covers how to check your number, how to read your email results, and how to write an email people answer.

## Quick Tips From the Trenches

### 1. Watch conversations per 100 dials, by phone number

Keep one weekly count for each number you call from: conversations divided by dials, times 100. A slow drift down is normal. A sharp drop in a single week, on the same kind of leads, usually means a carrier has started labeling your number. When that happens, test it on Verizon, T-Mobile and AT&T before you buy new leads or rewrite your script. You may be about to fix the wrong problem.

### 2. Write emails that ask one question

The email that gets replies reads like a note from a person: one or two sentences and a single question the reader can answer in a few words. "Did you ever get your burial coverage sorted out?" gets answers. "I will be calling you Tuesday at 2" doesn't, because there's nothing to answer. Leave out the logo, the product list and the rate table. The reply is the goal. A reply is also the best reason you'll ever have to call.

### 3. Sort Monday's list by what each lead did

Before you dial, split your leads into four groups: replied, clicked, opened only, and did nothing. Call in that order. Replies and clicks get a call the same day. Opens get a call, but don't trust them too much, because Apple Mail records many opens automatically when nobody read the email. Leads who did nothing get two calls at most and stay on your monthly email. You'll make fewer calls, and more of them will be answered.

## Industry Insight

**Only 14% of people say they immediately answer an unknown number**

Hiya's 2026 State of the Call report, a survey of more than 12,000 consumers in six countries, found that only 14% immediately answer calls from numbers they don't know. Pew Research found the same pattern in the U.S. in 2020: about 8 in 10 adults don't generally answer unknown cellphone calls. The answer rate on a cold dial has a hard ceiling. Leads who have already read your email or replied to it are the ones above that ceiling. Hiya is a call-screening company, so read its figure as an industry survey, not a neutral census.

Sources: https://www.hiya.com/state-of-the-call · https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2020/12/14/most-americans-dont-answer-cellphone-calls-from-unknown-numbers/
